# Lesson 1: Where Does the Energy Go?

## Unit
Energy: Stores and Transfers

## Lesson focus
Students learn to describe any change as energy moving between stores. They name the stores, the four ways energy is transferred, and what a system is, then describe five familiar changes.

## Why this lesson comes first
Every later lesson calculates the energy in one store: kinetic, gravitational or elastic. Students need a reason to calculate it. Here, they learn that energy is never made or used up; it only moves. That is the idea the calculations in Lessons 2–6 test, and the idea that wasted energy and efficiency in Lessons 8–9 depend on.

## Specification
AQA GCSE Physics 4.1.1.1 Energy stores and systems.

## Learning objectives
By the end of the lesson, students should be able to:

1. Name the energy stores and give an example of each.
2. Name the four ways energy is transferred between stores.
3. Explain what is meant by a system.
4. Describe the changes in energy stores when a ball is thrown upwards, when a fireplace warms a person, when a force speeds an object up, when a vehicle slows down and when a child lands on a trampoline.

## Key vocabulary

| Keyword | Student-friendly definition |
|---|---|
| Energy | What must be transferred to make something change; measured in joules (J) |
| Energy store | Where energy is held; the main stores are kinetic, gravitational potential, elastic potential, thermal and chemical |
| Transfer | Energy moving from one store to another |
| System | The object or group of objects we choose to study |
| Closed system | A system that no energy enters or leaves; its total energy stays the same |
| Joule (J) | The unit of energy |

## The stores

| Store | Energy is held because… | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Kinetic | an object is moving | A rolling football |
| Gravitational potential | an object is raised in a gravitational field | A book on a high shelf |
| Elastic potential | an object is stretched or squashed | A drawn bow, a stretched spring |
| Thermal | an object is hot (its particles move faster) | A cup of tea |
| Chemical | of the chemical bonds in a substance | Food, fuel, a battery |

These five are the stores GCSE questions use. Mention the magnetic (two fridge magnets held apart), electrostatic (a charged balloon near a wall) and nuclear (uranium fuel) stores so that students recognise the names, but do not drill them; nuclear fuel returns with energy resources in Year 10.

## The four ways energy is transferred

| Pathway | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanically | A force moves something | Pushing a trolley |
| Electrically | A current flows | A battery lighting a lamp |
| By heating | A hotter object warms a cooler one | A hob heating a pan |
| By waves | A wave carries it: light, infrared or sound | The Sun warming your face; a loudspeaker |

"By waves" puts light, infrared, sound and water waves in one pathway, which leads into wave power and the Waves unit. Light and infrared are electromagnetic radiation, and exam questions may say "by radiation": tell students that for light and infrared the two names mean the same pathway.

Key point:

> Stores hold energy. Transfers move it. "Light energy" and "sound energy" are not stores: they are ways energy travels from one store to another.

## Starter: What has changed?

Show a photograph or short clip: a skateboarder rolling up a ramp, stopping, then rolling back down.

Ask students:

1. What changes as the skateboarder goes up?
2. Where was the "go" when they stopped at the top?
3. Where does it come from on the way down?
4. Why do they not quite get back to their starting height?

Accept everyday language. Do not correct it yet; return to these answers at the end of the lesson.

## Main teaching: Stores, transfers and systems

Introduce the five main stores with one familiar example each, name the other three, then the four pathways. Use the table above.

Then introduce the system. The same event can be described differently depending on what you include:

- If the system is just a falling ball, gravity does work on it from outside.
- If the system is the ball and the Earth, the energy simply moves from the gravitational store to the kinetic store inside the system.

Students only need the idea: a system is whatever we choose to look at, and in a closed system the total energy stays the same.

## Main teaching: Five changes

Work through each case. Write the stores before and after, then the pathway.

| Change | Before | After | Pathway |
|---|---|---|---|
| A ball thrown upwards | Kinetic | Gravitational potential | Mechanically (gravity slows it) |
| A fireplace warms a person | Chemical (fuel) | Thermal (person) | By waves (infrared) |
| A trolley pushed with a constant force | Chemical (in the pusher's muscles) | Kinetic (and thermal) | Mechanically |
| A car braking | Kinetic | Thermal (brakes and surroundings) | Mechanically (friction) |
| A child lands on a trampoline | Kinetic (child) | Elastic potential (trampoline) | Mechanically |

For the fireplace, focus on someone warmed by radiation from a distance. Burning fuel heats the fire, which emits infrared waves. The fire also heats the surrounding air.

For the trampoline, focus on the landing: the child slows down as the mat and springs stretch. During the fall before landing, energy transfers from the gravitational potential store to the kinetic store.

Teach students to write it as a sentence:

> Energy is transferred mechanically from the kinetic store of the car to the thermal store of the brakes.

## Activity 1: Energy transfers cartoon

Students annotate a six-frame cartoon (in the workbook) with the store that is filling and the store that is emptying in each frame, and the pathway between frames. Suggested story: a child eats breakfast, cycles up a hill, freewheels down, brakes at the bottom.

| Frame | Main store emptying | Main store filling |
|---|---|---|
| Eating | Chemical (food) | Chemical (body) |
| Cycling uphill | Chemical (body) | Gravitational potential, thermal |
| Freewheeling down | Gravitational potential | Kinetic |
| Braking | Kinetic | Thermal (brakes) |

## Activity 2: Energy circus (optional)

Four or five stations around the room. At each, students carry out the change and write the stores before and after.

- Wind-up toy: elastic potential → kinetic
- Dropping a ball onto the bench: gravitational potential → kinetic → thermal store of the ball, the bench and the surroundings (some carried by sound waves)
- Rubbing hands: chemical → kinetic → thermal
- Battery and lamp: chemical → (electrically) → (by waves and by heating) → thermal store of the surroundings
- Squeezing a stress ball and letting go: chemical (muscles) → elastic potential → kinetic and thermal

## Check for understanding

1. Name three energy stores.
2. Is light a store or a transfer?
3. What is the unit of energy?
4. What happens to the energy of a car when it brakes?
5. A ball is thrown upwards. Which store empties and which fills?
6. What is a closed system?

## Plenary

Return to the skateboarder. Students now rewrite the starter answers in energy language:

> On the way up, energy is transferred from the kinetic store to the gravitational potential store. On the way down, it goes back. Some is transferred to the thermal store of the wheels and the surroundings each time, so they never get quite as high again.

The last sentence is a preview of Lesson 8.

## Possible prep

For three activities at home (for example, stretching a rubber band, riding a bike downhill, bouncing a ball), write which store empties, which fills and the pathway.

## Teacher notes

Common misconceptions:

1. **Energy is "used up".** It never is. It moves to a store where it is less useful, usually the thermal store of the surroundings. This is the most important correction in the unit.
2. **Light, sound and electricity are stores.** They are pathways. Insist on "transferred electrically", not "electrical energy".
3. **Energy and force are the same thing.** A force can transfer energy, but a force is a push or a pull measured in newtons; energy is measured in joules.

AQA does not test the list of stores by recall in isolation, but students must use the language accurately in every longer answer in the unit.
