# KeyFire

A typing game in the spirit of rail shooters: the player rides through five stages and types physics words to shoot down what jumps out. Its page is `fun/keyfire.html`; like everything in `fun/` it is network-only. It needs no build step, and the files share the `?v=` value `16`: bump it in `fun/keyfire.html` and in every import when the files change, so a new page cannot load incompatible browser-cached code. The UI, terms and definitions use British English; typed answers accept British and American spellings (`src/prompt-utils.js` lists the variants).

Run the tests with `node --test fun/keyfire/tests/*.test.mjs`.

`stage-preview.html` is a development page: it builds any wave's stage, flies through it, jumps between its stops and shows the hiding spots, the triangle count and the draw calls. Its lighting is approximate, without the game's sky, weather and post-processing.

## How it works

- **On rails.** `src/stages.js` builds a new stage for each wave: a winding path through a valley, a stop for each ambush and a boss arena. The game never moves its camera. Instead it moves and turns `stageRig` so the current point on the route sits at the origin, facing −z. At a stop, the stop's frame is the game's frame, so hiding spots are written in game coordinates.
- **Chapters.** A run is five stages, one per place (the code calls them waves; the player sees "Stage"). `src/chapters.js` gives each its palette, its scenery kit from `src/stage-kits.js` and its boss: a terraced spring orchard and meadow (Verdant Colossus), a summer forest with a river, a mill and a bridge (Crimson Bulwark), an autumn mountain pass whose route walks through the mouth of a torch-lit crystal mine (Magma Sovereign), a frozen lake under an aurora leading to a storm tower (Glacial Titan), and floating islands in the night sky (Stellar Dreadnought). A kit shapes and colours the land beside the route, supplies the cover and builds the props and the boss arena's landmark; it may also ask for a crossing (a river, a chasm, a gap between islands), which lengthens one leg of the route, and for a late part with a roof and its own palette, which is how the pass becomes the mine. A new palette arrives through a brief fog bank. Kits add no lights: glows are emissive materials. The difficulty tables were tuned over ten steps; the five waves climb them two at a time (`monsterLevel`, `bossLevel` in `src/main.js`).
- **Ambushes.** The rail section of `src/main.js` splits the wave's monsters, healers and chests into bursts, one per stop. Monsters jump out from behind cover, rise from the ground or drop from the sky, then rush to `STRIKE_Z`, wind up and strike. Each one's walk time comes from an estimate of how long the player needs for its word and for every word already on screen. Healers and chests run across the stop instead, on a run kept clear of the stage's cottages, trees and rocks (`pathClear` in `src/stages.js`); a chest turns part way across and charges (`startMimicCharge`). When the wave's typing budget runs out before its stops do, each stop left over is a breather: one or two easy monsters with short words and a chest, outside the wave's count.
- **Life.** The player has three hearts (`HEART_COUNT` in `src/main.js`), counted in halves. A plain monster that gets through takes a whole heart; casters' bolts, splitter halves, summoned imps, cart fliers, boss rocks and a mimic's bite take half; the last boss's beam takes a whole heart. Healers give life back only when their word is typed as they cross: a small healer, the heart at about a third of its size with a 4–7 letter word, gives half a heart; a big one, the full-size heart with a word of 8 letters or more, a whole heart. From Stage 2 each healer has a one in three chance of being big (`BIG_HEALER_CHANCE`). In a boss fight a small healer crosses the arena once in each stage of the fight, a little after the player has lost life (`updateBossHealer`). Every chest is a mimic, its lid snapping on its teeth as it runs. Part way across (`MIMIC_TURN_AT`) it turns and charges like a monster: typed in time it drops loot, but if it reaches the player it bites for half a heart and breaks the streak, unless the shield takes it.
- **Variants.** From wave 2 some monsters behave differently (`VARIANT_COUNTS` in `src/difficulty.js`, parts in `src/monster-variants.js`). A shield-bearer needs two short words: the first breaks its shield and drives it back. A splitter breaks into two small monsters with words of three or four letters. A caster stands back and lobs a bolt every few seconds, half a heart each, until its long word is typed; a bolt in the air fizzles when its caster falls.
- **Set-pieces.** Each chapter after the meadow has a moment of its own (`setPiece` in `src/chapters.js`, the set-pieces section of `src/main.js`). River wood: the walk stops on the bridge and a few plain monsters climb over its rails, two at most at a time. Mountain: once inside the mine the camera rides a cart that never stops (`src/ride-cart.js` draws its nose in front of the view, around the cannon), and cave fliers with short words rush at it; the leg into the mine is longer to make room (`RIDE_EXTRA` in `src/stages.js`). Frozen lake: the second ambush is fought in a blizzard of drifting flakes, where a monster's card stays hidden until it is close. Night sky: every monster flies, and two of its stops are challenges, the second and the last before the boss: a swarm of space bats marked with units. A card first says what it takes; then bats keep coming, up to five at a time and spread over the sky (`BAT_SPOTS`), and fifteen brought down in half a minute, twenty in the second swarm (`BAT_GOALS`), earn points. The bridge fight and the cart ride each take one of the wave's ambushes.
- **Sound.** `src/audio.js` synthesises everything with the Web Audio API, no samples, as dark driving synth. Each wave has its own track with its own key, tempo, rhythm, drum kit and lead (a bright 4/4 for the meadow, rolling triplets for the river wood, a heavy half-time for the mine, sparse and glassy for the frozen lake, the fastest for the night sky), and each boss plays its own wave's track intensified. The cannon is a plasma gun: a low thump with a crackle on every letter and a charged blast on the kill. Every kind of monster has a voice, heard as it shows itself and as it dies (`CREATURE_VOICES`), as do the healer and the chest; each boss roars, throws, cries out and dies in the sound of what it is made of: timber, armour, lava, ice, machinery (`BOSS_VOICES`).
- **Story.** `src/story.js` holds the story and `src/storyteller.js` builds the five storytellers for both the encounters and the ending. Each role supplies its silhouette, costume and props. In the story, the land is overrun by myths, monsters born of bad science that only true physics words destroy. One stop in each wave has no ambush (`rail.storyIndex`): someone waits there and speaks a few lines in a card, typed out letter by letter with a blip of their voice. Space shows the rest of a line at once; a finished line waits, with a pulsing Space key, for Space to move on, and Skip ends the story once nothing holds the storyteller. From wave 2 a myth holds them first and they cry "Help! Help! Help!" in a bubble above their head, so the first line is not missed while the player types; the bubble stacks with the word cards so it never covers one. Typing the myth's word frees them. Done talking, they turn and run off to the right.
- **Keyboard only.** Phones and tablets with no fine pointer see a "Play on a Computer" card instead of the start screen (`setUpDeviceGate` in `src/main.js`). A key press from a physical keyboard, such as an iPad keyboard case, dismisses it and is remembered under `keyfireHasKeyboard`.
- **Typing.** The input bar is gone: the letters typed so far light up in the target's own word, as in the boss's lines (typed on another accepted spelling, the card shows that spelling). No word is locked: a letter that does not continue the current word but starts another switches to that one, so a rock can be shot down in the middle of a long word. A key that fits nothing is only a miss: it costs accuracy and the chain, but the letters typed so far and the target stay.
- **Words and units.** Monsters, healers and chests carry vocabulary words only. Short picks (cart fliers, shields, breather monsters) draw on every short word in the bank, whatever its tier, so they seldom repeat. The plain-letter unit symbols in `ROCK_UNITS` (`src/question-bank.js`) mark the rocks a boss throws. Typing ignores capitals everywhere.
- **The plasma cannon.** `src/plasma-cannon.js` hangs the player's weapon from the camera low and a little right of the middle, angled in toward the centre so only its top rises into view: a plasma machine gun whose six barrels spin up as the player types. It turns toward the target, kicks back and flashes on every letter (bolts leave from its muzzle), and chain lightning leaps from the crystal core on top. It shrinks and moves in on narrow screens and adds no lights.
- **Ranks.** Each monster, healer or chest beaten shows a rank from S down to E, slammed in over a flash ring, by letters a second from the first letter typed of its word to the last, in real time, so a time freeze does not flatter it (`RANK_SPEEDS` in `src/main.js`). A word of one letter is not ranked. Each rank adds a bonus for every letter of the word (`RANK_BONUS_PER_LETTER`: 12 for S down to none for E), shown under the letter, and the summary card after the ending counts the words that earned each rank. A boss is ranked for each word of a word stage and each finished statement or story line. Rocks are not ranked.
- **Leaderboard.** `src/leaderboard.js` is the High Scores board, the top ten scores with no placeholder rows (an empty board invites the first score). It shows before and after a run and while paused; the HUD shows only the score and the life meter. See "Leaderboard" below.
- **Question bank.** `src/question-bank.js` covers high school physics, not only GCSE: vocabulary in three bands, boss statements in `BOSS_PHRASES` and physicist stories in `BOSS_STORIES`.
- **Feel.** Every correct letter flicks a bolt from the cannon and makes the monster flinch. A finishing letter adds a hit pause and a camera kick, and the last kill of an ambush plays in slow motion.
- **Detail.** `src/detail.js` sets how finely the world is faceted: still flat-shaded, but round shapes, leaf clumps, rocks, clouds and the ground are cut into more, smaller facets. Battery saver keeps the coarse low-poly shapes: toggling it rebuilds the stage in place from the same seed, so only the facets change.
- **Bosses.** Each wave ends with one boss in the arena, fought in stages (`BOSS_PLAN_TABLE` and `bossFightOrder` in `src/difficulty.js`). The first four start with stages of single words, shown one at a time under the health bar and typed like monsters' words. Each stage ends on a long term, a phrase too long for any monster (more than `LONGEST_MONSTER_WORD` typed letters, such as "specific heat capacity"), shown whole and typed word by word; `longTerm` in the table caps its length, which grows from boss to boss. Then they deal the finishing blow: one whole statement from `BOSS_PHRASES` in `src/question-bank.js`, two for the fourth boss. Each stage, statement or story line takes a segment off the health bar. Between words the player can shatter what the boss throws, and each boss attacks in its own way (`BOSS_ATTACKS` in `src/main.js`): single rocks, a rock from each hand, a summoned imp, or shards of ice that frost over its line. The last boss, the Stellar Dreadnought, changes weapon with each stage (`dreadnoughtAttack`): comets during four words, comets that split in two if left past halfway during one statement, then three orbiting satellites that shield its core word and fire bolts until they are shot down, and finally a four-line story about a physicist from `BOSS_STORIES`, with a plain comet now and then and a beam that charges over each line (`beamChargeTime`) and fires for a heart if the line is not finished in time. At each change of weapon a big healer crosses the arena. For testing, the Konami code skips ahead one step each time it is entered: from a stage to its boss, from a boss to the next stage, and from the last boss to the ending; the run stays unranked. A boss prowls, roars, rears back to throw and reels when hurt; beaten, it shudders and bursts apart.
- **Ending.** Beating the last boss plays a 42-second film (`src/ending-cinematic.js`): the Dreadnought breaks and its myth rises, the run's defeated words charge the cannon, one beam breaks the myth, a wave of light frees the five lands and their storytellers in turn, and the five gather at dawn beneath “Thank you for playing!”. The orchard and river shots use the same mill models as the playable stages. Everything in it is a function of one clock (`ENDING_TIMELINE`, a 144 bpm bar grid), which is the audio clock when sound is on; the score in `src/audio.js` (`buildVictoryScore`) is written on the same grid, so change the two together. The summary card (`#gameEnding` in the page) follows, and Skip jumps straight to it.

## Leaderboard

- Scores are read from the `keyfire_leaderboard` table and written only through the `submit_keyfire_score` function, which also limits each network address to five submissions a minute (`keyfire_submit_log`). The table, the function and the score-range check live in the Supabase project "PanPhy Games", not in this repository.
- `MAX_SCORE` in `src/leaderboard.js` must match the server's range check (`keyfire_score_range`). Raise both together if a scoring change makes a legitimate run exceed it, or high scores will be rejected.
- A run that used a cheat is never submitted. God mode shows "GOD MODE" low in the middle of the view and gilds the cannon (`setGolden` in `src/plasma-cannon.js`).
- **Reset the board after any gameplay change** (speed, timing, damage, scoring, ranks, stages, bosses or the question bank), so old scores never compete with new rules. Once the change is live: copy the table into the `backup` schema, which the API does not expose (`create table backup.keyfire_leaderboard_YYYYMMDD as table public.keyfire_leaderboard;`), check the row counts match, then `delete from public.keyfire_leaderboard;`. In the same pull request as the gameplay change, bump `CACHE_KEY` in `src/leaderboard.js` and add the old key to `OLD_CACHE_KEYS`, so browsers drop their saved copy of the old board. To restore a backup: `insert into public.keyfire_leaderboard select * from backup.keyfire_leaderboard_YYYYMMDD;`.
- The page loads the Supabase client from jsDelivr and uses plain REST calls if it did not load. The last list fetched is kept in localStorage and shown while the live one loads; the status reads "Offline" when the fetch fails.
- `.github/workflows/supabase-keepalive.yml` queries this table and Asteroid Storm's `dodge_leaderboard` twice a week so that the free-tier project is not paused. It carries a copy of the public anon key, so update it there too if the key or project changes.
