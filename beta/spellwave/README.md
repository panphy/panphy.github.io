# Spellwave (beta rework)

A working copy of Spellwave (`fun/spellwave.html`) for reworking it in the spirit of rail-shooter typing games. The live game is untouched; this copy has a leaderboard of its own and keeps its best score and word history under its own localStorage keys. The page is `beta/spellwave.html`. It needs no build step, and the files share the `?v=` value `beta26`: bump it in `beta/spellwave.html` and in every import when the files change.

Run the tests with `node --test beta/spellwave/tests/*.test.mjs`.

`stage-preview.html` is a development page: it builds any wave's stage, flies through it, jumps between its stops and shows the hiding spots, the triangle count and the draw calls. Its lighting is approximate, without the game's sky, weather and post-processing.

## What differs from the live game

- **On rails.** `src/stages.js` builds a new stage for each wave: a winding path through a valley, a stop for each ambush and a boss arena. The game never moves its camera. Instead it moves and turns `stageRig` so the current point on the route sits at the origin, facing −z. At a stop, the stop's frame is the game's frame, so hiding spots are written in game coordinates.
- **Chapters.** A run is five stages, one per place (the code calls them waves; the player sees "Stage"). `src/chapters.js` gives each its palette, its scenery kit from `src/stage-kits.js` and its boss: a spring meadow (Verdant Colossus), a summer forest with a river, a mill and a bridge (Crimson Bulwark), an autumn mountain pass whose route walks through the mouth of a torch-lit crystal mine (Magma Sovereign), a frozen lake leading to a storm tower (Glacial Titan), and floating islands in the night sky (Stellar Dreadnought). A kit shapes and colours the land beside the route, supplies the cover and builds the props and the boss arena's landmark; it may also ask for a crossing (a river, a chasm, a gap between islands), which lengthens one leg of the route, and for a late part with a roof and its own palette, which is how the pass becomes the mine. A new palette arrives through a brief fog bank. Kits add no lights: glows are emissive materials. The difficulty tables were tuned over ten steps; the five waves climb them two at a time (`monsterLevel`, `bossLevel` in `src/main.js`).
- **Ambushes.** The rail section of `src/main.js` splits the wave's monsters, healers and chests into bursts, one per stop. Monsters jump out from behind cover, rise from the ground or drop from the sky, then rush to `STRIKE_Z`, wind up and strike. Each one's walk time comes from an estimate of how long the player needs for its word and for every word already on screen. Healers and chests run across the stop instead.
- **Variants.** From wave 2 some monsters behave differently (`VARIANT_COUNTS` in `src/difficulty.js`, parts in `src/monster-variants.js`). A shield-bearer needs two short words: the first breaks its shield and drives it back. A splitter breaks into two small monsters with words of three or four letters. A caster stands back and lobs a bolt every few seconds, half a heart each, until its long word is typed; a bolt in the air fizzles when its caster falls.
- **Set-pieces.** Each chapter after the meadow has a moment of its own (`setPiece` in `src/chapters.js`, the set-pieces section of `src/main.js`). River wood: the walk stops on the bridge and a few plain monsters climb over its rails, two at most at a time. Mountain: once inside the mine the camera rides a cart that never stops, and cave fliers with short words rush at it; the leg into the mine is longer to make room (`RIDE_EXTRA` in `src/stages.js`). Frozen lake: the second ambush is fought in a blizzard of drifting flakes, where a monster's card stays hidden until it is close. Night sky: every monster flies, and its second stop is a challenge, a swarm of space bats marked with units: ten to bring down in half a minute, for points and a heart. The bridge fight and the cart ride each take one of the wave's ambushes.
- **Sound.** `src/audio.js` synthesises everything with the Web Audio API, no samples, as dark driving synth. Each wave has its own track with its own key, tempo, rhythm, drum kit and lead (a bright 4/4 for the meadow, rolling triplets for the river wood, a heavy half-time for the mine, sparse and glassy for the frozen lake, the fastest for the night sky), and each boss plays its own wave's track intensified. The cannon is a plasma gun: a low thump with a crackle on every letter and a charged blast on the kill.
- **Story.** `src/story.js` holds the story and `src/storyteller.js` builds the people who tell it: the land is overrun by myths, monsters born of bad science that only true physics words destroy. One stop in each wave has no ambush (`rail.storyIndex`): someone waits there and speaks a few lines in a card, typed out letter by letter with a blip of their voice; holding space hurries them along. From wave 2 a myth holds them first, and typing its word frees them and earns a heart.
- **Typing.** The input bar is gone: what the player types shows under the word in the target's own card. No word is locked: a letter that does not continue the current word but starts another switches to that one, so a rock can be shot down in the middle of a long word. A key that fits nothing is only a miss: it costs accuracy and the chain, but the letters typed so far and the target stay. The typed letters are blue, apart from the monster's word in ink.
- **Words and units.** Monsters, healers and chests carry vocabulary words only. The plain-letter unit symbols in `ROCK_UNITS` (`src/question-bank.js`) mark the rocks a boss throws. Typing ignores capitals everywhere.
- **The arcane cannon.** `src/arcane-cannon.js` hangs the player's weapon from the camera low and a little right of the middle, angled in toward the centre so only its top rises into view: a magic machine gun whose six barrels spin up as the player types. It turns toward the target, kicks back and flashes on every letter (bolts leave from its muzzle), and chain lightning leaps from the crystal core on top. It shrinks and moves in on narrow screens and adds no lights.
- **Ranks.** Each monster, healer or chest beaten shows a rank from S down to E, slammed in over a flash ring, by letters a second from the first letter typed at it to the last (`RANK_SPEEDS` in `src/main.js`). A boss is ranked for each word of a word stage and each finished statement or story line. Rocks are not ranked.
- **Leaderboard.** `src/leaderboard.js` is the Hall of Wizards for the beta: its own Supabase table and submit function (`spellwave_beta_leaderboard`, `submit_spellwave_beta_score`), so beta scores never reach the live game's board. It shows before and after a run and while paused. After a gameplay change, reset it as `fun/spellwave/README.md` describes for the live board, with the beta's names, and bump `CACHE_KEY`. The HUD shows only the score and the life meter.
- **Question bank.** `src/question-bank.js` covers high school physics, not only GCSE: vocabulary in three bands, boss statements in `BOSS_PHRASES` and physicist stories in `BOSS_STORIES`.
- **Feel.** Every correct letter flicks a bolt from the wand and makes the monster flinch. A finishing letter adds a hit pause and a camera kick, and the last kill of an ambush plays in slow motion.

- **Bosses.** Each wave ends with one boss in the arena, fought in stages (`BOSS_PLAN_TABLE` in `src/difficulty.js`). First come stages of single words, shown one at a time under the health bar and typed like monsters' words. Then the finishing blow: one whole statement from `BOSS_PHRASES` in `src/question-bank.js`, two for the fourth boss. The last boss ends instead with a four-line story about a physicist from `BOSS_STORIES`. Each stage, statement or story line takes a segment off the health bar. Between words the player can shatter what the boss throws, and each boss attacks in its own way (`BOSS_ATTACKS` in `src/main.js`): single rocks, a rock from each hand, a summoned imp, shards of ice that frost over its line, or three meteors in a row. A boss prowls, roars, rears back to throw and reels when hurt; beaten, it shudders and bursts apart.
- **Ending.** Beating the last boss plays a 42-second film (`src/ending-cinematic.js`): the Dreadnought breaks and its myth rises, the run's defeated words charge the cannon, one beam breaks the myth, a wave of light frees the five lands and their storytellers in turn, and the five gather at dawn under the game's name. Everything in it is a function of one clock (`ENDING_TIMELINE`, a 144 bpm bar grid), which is the audio clock when sound is on; the score in `src/audio.js` (`buildVictoryScore`) is written on the same grid, so change the two together. The summary card (`#gameEnding` in the page) follows, and Skip jumps straight to it.
