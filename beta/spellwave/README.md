# Spellwave (beta rework)

A working copy of Spellwave (`fun/spellwave.html`) for reworking it in the spirit of rail-shooter typing games. The live game is untouched; this copy has no leaderboard (`src/leaderboard.js` is a stub) and keeps its best score and word history under its own localStorage keys. The page is `beta/spellwave.html`. It needs no build step, and the files share the `?v=` value `beta10`: bump it in `beta/spellwave.html` and in every import when the files change.

Run the tests with `node --test beta/spellwave/tests/*.test.mjs`.

`stage-preview.html` is a development page: it builds any wave's stage, flies through it, jumps between its stops and shows the hiding spots, the triangle count and the draw calls. Its lighting is approximate, without the game's sky, weather and post-processing.

## What differs from the live game

- **On rails.** `src/stages.js` builds a new stage for each wave: a winding path through a valley, a stop for each ambush and a boss arena. The game never moves its camera. Instead it moves and turns `stageRig` so the current point on the route sits at the origin, facing −z. At a stop, the stop's frame is the game's frame, so hiding spots are written in game coordinates.
- **Chapters.** `src/chapters.js` gives each pair of waves a place, a palette and a scenery kit from `src/stage-kits.js`: a spring meadow, a summer forest with a river, a mill and a bridge, an autumn mountain pass that leads into a torch-lit crystal mine, a frozen lake under a storm tower, and floating islands in the night sky. A kit shapes and colours the land beside the route, supplies the cover and builds the props and the boss arena's landmark; it may also ask for a crossing (a river, a chasm, a gap between islands), which lengthens one leg of the route, and for a roof. A new palette arrives through a brief fog bank. Kits add no lights: glows are emissive materials.
- **Ambushes.** The rail section of `src/main.js` splits the wave's monsters, healers and chests into bursts, one per stop. Monsters jump out from behind cover, rise from the ground or drop from the sky, then rush to `STRIKE_Z`, wind up and strike. Each one's walk time comes from an estimate of how long the player needs for its word and for every word already on screen. Healers and chests run across the stop instead.
- **Typing.** The input bar is gone: what the player types shows under the word in the target's own card. A wrong key is only a miss. It costs accuracy and the chain, but the letters typed so far and the target stay. The typed letters are blue, apart from the monster's word in ink.
- **Words and units.** Monsters, healers and chests carry vocabulary words only. The plain-letter unit symbols in `ROCK_UNITS` (`src/question-bank.js`) mark the rocks a boss throws. Typing ignores capitals everywhere.
- **The wizard's hands.** `src/wizard-hands.js` hangs two hands from the camera in the bottom corners: the right holds a wand that turns toward the target and flicks on every letter (bolts leave from its tip), the left is open with a rune over the palm (chain lightning leaps from it). They shrink and move in on narrow screens, and add no lights.
- **Feel.** Every correct letter flicks a bolt from the wand and makes the monster flinch. A finishing letter adds a hit pause and a camera kick, and the last kill of an ambush plays in slow motion.

- **Bosses.** Each wave ends with one boss in the arena. It speaks whole statements from `BOSS_PHRASES` in `src/question-bank.js`, shown under the health bar, clear of the rocks flying in; the letters typed turn blue in place. The player types them word by word, and each finished statement takes a segment off the health bar at the top. Between words the player can shatter the rocks it throws, each marked with a short unit; no rock starts with the same letter as the boss's next word or another rock. The ten boss models are dealt from a shuffled bag, so each wave of a run has a different one. `bossPlan` in `src/difficulty.js` sets the statements, the longest statement, the throw rate, the rock flight time and the rock damage for each wave. Wave 10 is a single eight-statement final boss. A line finished with chain lightning primed takes two segments and clears the rocks. The boss prowls the arena, roars between throws, rears back and lunges to throw, reels when a line lands and grows faster and closer as it is hurt; when it falls it shudders and bursts apart before the wave ends. Its model reads `enemy.windup`, `enemy.hurt` and `enemy.rage` in its `animate` function.
