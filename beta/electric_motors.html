A model is an explanation, not a photograph.

Real motors turn tens of times every second, so all the motion here is slowed down a great deal; the supply frequencies are shown slowed down too. Sizes, colours and arrow lengths are chosen to make the ideas visible. Force arrows grow with the size of the force but are not drawn to an exact scale.

The forces and torques in the readouts use real formulas and values: a 5.0 cm rod in the motor-effect view, and a coil with 4.0 cm long sides and an area of 20 cm² in the DC and AC views. The coil is drawn with one loop for every 10 turns. The motion uses a simple model of friction and air resistance, and the DC supply keeps the current steady; in a real motor the back emf makes the current fall as the motor speeds up. The AC motor’s coil includes a damper winding, as real synchronous motors do, which stops it swinging back and forth about the synchronous speed.

The induction motor uses a standard torque–slip curve with an exaggerated slip so you can see it; real motors usually slip by only a few per cent at full load. The field arrows show the direction of the combined field inside the motor, and the rotor bar colours show the induced currents in a simplified way.
