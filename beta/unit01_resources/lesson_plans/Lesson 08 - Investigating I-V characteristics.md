# Lesson 8: Investigating I-V characteristics

**Unit:** Electric Circuits · Year 10 · AQA GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy (Higher) / separate Physics
**Specification:** RP 16 / RP 4
**Length:** 50 minutes

## Why this lesson is here

Changing only the test component while keeping the measuring circuit clear lets you compare its electrical behaviour.

## Learning objective

Collect bipolar I-V data safely, graph it and justify the three shapes.

## Core teaching point

Vary p.d., record current, reverse the supply for negative readings. Keep a fixed resistor cool; protect the diode with a series resistor; do not exceed the lamp rating.

## Equipment and preparation

Variable low-voltage supply, variable resistor, meters, lamp, fixed resistor, diode, protective resistor, switch, leads; existing I-V worksheet pp. 1-8.

## Lesson sequence

| Minutes | Phase | Teacher move and evidence to collect |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-6 | Safety and prediction | Review lamp rating and diode protection. |
| 6-13 | Build | Check series ammeter and parallel voltmeter. |
| 13-34 | Required practical | Collect all three devices and both directions; rotate roles. |
| 34-44 | Graph | Plot characteristic curves; finish if needed. |
| 44-50 | Check | Name and explain each shape. |

## Workbook task guidance

### Task 1

Draw a series supply-switch-ammeter-variable resistor-test component loop and voltmeter across the test component.

**Expected:** Ammeter and variable resistor in series with test component; voltmeter across test component only.

### Task 2

Collect readings for fixed resistor, lamp and protected diode, including reverse direction. Record negative signs correctly.

**Expected:** Student-dependent readings; negative V and I in reverse direction. Use diode protection and lamp rating.

### Task 3

Plot I (y) against V (x) for each device and label the origin and units.

**Expected:** I vertical, V horizontal; put negative readings in the third quadrant.

### Task 4

Explain the shapes with constant temperature, filament heating and one-way diode conduction.

**Expected:** Fixed resistor: constant R; lamp heats, R rises; diode mainly conducts forward.

## Worked example

For a fixed resistor, V = +3.0 V and I = +0.15 A gives R = 20 ohm. Reversed readings may be -3.0 V and -0.15 A.

## AQA-style checkpoint

[6 marks] Describe a method to obtain the complete I-V characteristic of a filament lamp and state two ways the method changes for a diode.

**Mark guidance:** Award up to six: correct ammeter series and voltmeter parallel; vary p.d. with supply/variable resistor; record V and I pairs; reverse supply for negative half; keep below lamp rating; diode gets protective series resistor; smaller voltage steps/milliammeter for diode; plot I against V.

## Anticipated misconception

Reversing the supply changes both V and I signs; do not plot reversed data in the positive quadrant.

## Assessment and next step

Collect one sentence of reasoning and one complete calculation or graph label. Start the next lesson by reteaching the most common error; students who are secure should tackle a changed numerical context.

## Practical hand-off

Use the workbook practical record for data and graphing. The existing full AQA required-practical worksheet supplies the detailed method, circuit diagrams and additional analysis. Keep apparatus at low voltage and follow local lab safety rules.
