# Lesson 7: Reading I-V graphs

**Unit:** Electric Circuits · Year 10 · AQA GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy (Higher) / separate Physics
**Specification:** 6.2.1.4
**Length:** 50 minutes

## Why this lesson is here

Three components share the same axes but produce very different curves. Their shape tells you how their resistance behaves.

## Learning objective

Recognise characteristic graph shapes and explain what they show.

## Core teaching point

On I (vertical) against V (horizontal): fixed resistor is a straight line through origin at constant temperature; lamp curve flattens as it heats; diode conducts mainly one way.

## Equipment and preparation

Printed/sketched I-V axes, graph paper, optional lamp demonstration; existing I-V practical worksheet.

## Lesson sequence

| Minutes | Phase | Teacher move and evidence to collect |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-6 | Retrieval | Calculate R at a graph point. |
| 6-19 | Teach shapes | Sketch resistor, lamp and diode with axes specified. |
| 19-31 | Compare | Students sort unlabelled curves and justify each. |
| 31-43 | Practice | Tasks 2-4, including two-point calculation. |
| 43-50 | Check | Four-mark lamp question. |

## Workbook task guidance

### Task 1

On a blank set of axes, sketch and label the three characteristic shapes in both directions.

**Expected:** Resistor straight line through origin; lamp symmetric curve flattening at high |V|; diode near zero reverse current and steep forward rise.

### Task 2

Explain why a filament lamp graph becomes less steep as p.d. rises.

**Expected:** Greater current heats the filament; resistance rises, so extra V gives proportionally less current.

### Task 3

At 2.0 V a component carries 0.10 A; at 4.0 V it carries 0.15 A. Calculate R at each point and decide whether it is ohmic.

**Expected:** R1 = 20 ohm; R2 = 26.7 ohm, so not constant R.

### Task 4

Describe what reversing a diode does to its current.

**Expected:** Reverse current is very small compared with forward current (ignoring breakdown).

## Worked example

A graph point is V = 4.0 V, I = 0.20 A. At that point R = 4.0/0.20 = 20 ohm. Do not infer R is constant from one point.

## AQA-style checkpoint

[4 marks] A lamp carries 0.20 A at 2.0 V and 0.30 A at 6.0 V. Calculate resistance at each point and explain the change.

**Mark guidance:** 10 ohm (1); 20 ohm (1). Larger current heats filament (1), raising resistance (1).

## Anticipated misconception

For I on the y-axis, a flatter graph indicates a larger resistance; always check the axes.

## Assessment and next step

Collect one sentence of reasoning and one complete calculation or graph label. Start the next lesson by reteaching the most common error; students who are secure should tackle a changed numerical context.
