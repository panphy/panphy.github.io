# Lesson 11: Power and energy

**Unit:** Electric Circuits · Year 10 · AQA GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy (Higher) / separate Physics
**Specification:** 6.2.4.1-2
**Length:** 50 minutes

## Why this lesson is here

A kettle and a lamp use the same mains supply but transfer energy at different rates. Their power ratings tell you how fast.

## Learning objective

Choose the right equation, convert units and solve multi-step appliance problems.

## Core teaching point

Power is energy transferred per second. P = VI and P = I²R; E = Pt and E = QV. Convert kW to W and minutes/hours to seconds for joules.

## Equipment and preparation

Appliance rating labels, calculators and equation toolkit.

## Workbook task guidance

### Task 1

Choose an equation for each pair of known quantities: V and I; I and R; P and t; Q and V.

**Expected:** P = VI; P = I^2R; E = Pt; E = QV.

### Task 2

A 230 V heater draws 4.0 A. Calculate its power and energy in 5.0 min.

**Expected:** P = 920 W; t = 300 s; E = 276000 J.

### Task 3

A 3.0 A current passes a 20 ohm resistor. Calculate dissipated power; explain why the current is squared.

**Expected:** P = 3.0^2 x 20 = 180 W; doubling I quadruples heating at fixed R.

### Task 4

A device transfers 240 J while 40 C flows. Calculate its p.d.

**Expected:** V = E/Q = 240/40 = 6.0 V.

## Worked example

2.0 kW kettle for 3.0 min: P = 2000 W, t = 180 s, E = Pt = 360000 J.

## AQA-style checkpoint

[6 marks] A 2.2 kW kettle runs at 230 V for 150 s. Calculate current and energy transferred in joules. A second kettle draws twice the current through the same resistance: compare its heating power.

**Mark guidance:** P = 2200 W (1); I = P/V = 2200/230 = 9.6 A (2); E = Pt = 2200 x 150 = 330000 J (2); P = I²R so doubling I gives four times the heating power (1).

## Anticipated misconception

P = I²R, not IR; E = Pt needs W and s for J.

## Assessment and next step
