# Lesson 5: Series and parallel

**Unit:** Electric Circuits · Year 10 · AQA GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy (Higher) / separate Physics
**Specification:** 6.2.2
**Length:** 50 minutes

## Why this lesson is here

One broken lamp turns off a series chain. A parallel branch can keep the other lamp working.

## Learning objective

Solve series and parallel circuit questions with clear reasons.

## Core teaching point

Series: current equal, p.d.s add, resistances add. Parallel: p.d. equal across branches, branch currents add; total resistance falls when a branch is added.

## Equipment and preparation

Cells, identical lamps and two resistors, meters; optional PhET lab; mini whiteboards.

## Lesson sequence

| Minutes | Phase | Teacher move and evidence to collect |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-6 | Retrieve | Measure/recall source and component p.d.s from Lab 2. |
| 6-18 | Model | Compare loop and branch diagrams; derive the rules. |
| 18-31 | Build and test | Measure currents and p.d.s in both arrangements. |
| 31-43 | Calculate | Tasks 2-4, using a rule before each calculation. |
| 43-50 | Check | Five-mark question and targeted misconception check. |

## Workbook task guidance

### Task 1

Draw two lamps in series and two lamps in parallel. Mark junctions clearly.

**Expected:** Series: one loop. Parallel: two branches between the same junctions.

### Task 2

Calculate the total resistance and current for 3.0 ohm and 9.0 ohm in series across 12 V.

**Expected:** Rtotal = 12 ohm; I = 12/12 = 1.0 A.

### Task 3

Parallel branches carry 0.20 A and 0.35 A on a 6.0 V battery. State each branch p.d. and the supply current.

**Expected:** Each branch is 6.0 V; supply current = 0.55 A.

### Task 4

Explain, without a parallel-resistance formula, why adding a branch lowers total resistance.

**Expected:** Extra path increases supply current at fixed p.d., so V/I for the whole circuit decreases.

## Worked example

2.0 ohm and 4.0 ohm in series on 12 V: Rtotal = 6.0 ohm; I = 2.0 A; p.d.s = 4.0 V and 8.0 V.

## AQA-style checkpoint

[5 marks] A 4.0 ohm and an 8.0 ohm resistor are in series on 12 V. Find total resistance, current and p.d. across each. State one parallel-circuit rule.

**Mark guidance:** Rtotal = 12 ohm (1); I = 12/12 = 1.0 A (1); V1 = 4.0 V (1); V2 = 8.0 V (1); e.g. same p.d. across branches (1).

## Anticipated misconception

Current splits at a parallel junction; p.d. does not split between branches.

## Assessment and next step

Collect one sentence of reasoning and one complete calculation or graph label. Start the next lesson by reteaching the most common error; students who are secure should tackle a changed numerical context.
