# Lesson 12: The National Grid and exam clinic

**Unit:** Electric Circuits · Year 10 · AQA GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy (Higher) / separate Physics
**Specification:** 6.2.4.3; 6.2.1-4
**Length:** 50 minutes

## Why this lesson is here

A power station sends energy over long cables. A step-up transformer raises p.d. so the same power can be transmitted with a smaller current.

## Learning objective

Explain efficient transmission and tackle mixed AQA-style electricity questions.

## Core teaching point

For the same transmitted power, raising V lowers I (P = VI); smaller I greatly reduces cable heating (P = I²R). Step down before domestic use.

## Equipment and preparation

Grid flow diagram, calculators, equation toolkit, end-of-unit exam clinic.

## Workbook task guidance

### Task 1

Draw a flow from power station to step-up transformer, transmission cables, step-down transformer and home; label why each step matters.

**Expected:** Power station -> step-up -> high-p.d. cables -> step-down -> homes; note lower transmission current.

### Task 2

Use P = VI to compare currents for 1.0 MW transmitted at 10 kV and 100 kV; then compare I²R loss for the same cable R.

**Expected:** I = 100 A at 10 kV and 10 A at 100 kV; with same R, cable loss falls by factor 100.

### Task 3

HT: calculate secondary current for an ideal transformer with 20 kV, 500 A input and 200 kV output.

**Expected:** HT: Is = (20000 x 500)/200000 = 50 A.

### Task 4

Start the end-of-unit exam clinic in class. Complete remaining questions at home, then correct in a different colour using the mark guidance.

**Expected:** Use the exam clinic marking notes after completing all questions; record one content and one technique target.

## Worked example

HT: ideal transformer has 25 kV, 400 A in and 400 kV out. Is = VpIp/Vs = 25 A.

## AQA-style checkpoint

[4 marks] Explain why the National Grid transmits electricity at high p.d. and why the p.d. is reduced before homes.

**Mark guidance:** Step-up raises p.d. (1); for fixed power, current falls (1); smaller I means much less I²R heating/loss in cables (1); step-down gives lower p.d. suitable for consumers (1).

## Anticipated misconception

A step-up transformer changes p.d. and current; it does not create extra power.

## Assessment and next step

Collect one sentence of reasoning and one complete calculation or graph label. Start the next lesson by reteaching the most common error; students who are secure should tackle a changed numerical context.

## Exam clinic mark guide

- 1: 2.5 min = 150 s; Q = It = 0.80 x 150 = 120 C (3).
- 2: V = E/Q = 48/8.0 = 6.0 V; 6 J transferred per coulomb (4).
- 3: Rtotal = 12 ohm; I = 1.0 A; each p.d. = 6.0 V (4).
- 4: Contact position/reading error, heating or poor connection; repeat point, check apparatus, compare with trend (any well-justified four points).
- 5: R = 10 ohm then 20 ohm; current heats filament and its resistance increases (4).
- 6: Earth connects metal case to 0 V via low-resistance path; fault gives large current; fuse melts/breaker trips and disconnects live, protecting user (5).
- 7: P = 1500 W, t = 240 s, E = 360000 J; I = P/V = 1500/230 = 6.5 A (5).
- 8: High V lowers I for fixed P (P=VI); lower I reduces I^2R heating; step down for users. HT: Is = (20000 x 500)/200000 = 50 A (7).
