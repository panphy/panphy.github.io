# Lesson 9: Sensors and circuit choices

**Unit:** Electric Circuits · Year 10 · AQA GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy (Higher) / separate Physics
**Specification:** 6.2.1.4, 6.2.2
**Length:** 50 minutes

## Why this lesson is here

An automatic night light and a temperature alarm use resistors whose resistance responds to the surroundings.

## Learning objective

Predict how light and temperature change current in simple sensor circuits.

## Core teaching point

NTC thermistor: temperature up, resistance down. LDR: light intensity up, resistance down. At fixed supply p.d., lower total resistance means higher current.

## Equipment and preparation

LDR, thermistor and multimeter for demo; graph cards; optional virtual circuit for series response.

## Lesson sequence

| Minutes | Phase | Teacher move and evidence to collect |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-5 | Retrieval | Recall lamp/resistor/diode characteristics. |

## Workbook task guidance

### Task 1

Sketch R against temperature for an NTC thermistor and R against light intensity for an LDR.

**Expected:** Both resistance curves fall as x variable rises; label axes and units/context.

### Task 2

Explain the current change in a series circuit containing a thermistor when it gets warmer.

**Expected:** Warmer NTC thermistor has lower R; total R and series current change accordingly at fixed V.

### Task 3

Calculate I for a 12 V sensor circuit with total R = 300 ohm, then 600 ohm.

**Expected:** 0.040 A then 0.020 A.

### Task 4

Choose thermistor or LDR for a fire warning and a streetlamp; justify each choice.

**Expected:** Thermistor for temperature/fire warning; LDR for light/streetlamp.

## Worked example

An LDR circuit has 6.0 V and total R = 600 ohm in bright light: I = 6.0/600 = 0.010 A. In dark R = 1200 ohm: I = 0.0050 A.

## AQA-style checkpoint

[4 marks] A 6.0 V supply is connected to an LDR and a fixed resistor in series. In dim light the total resistance is 1500 ohm. Calculate current. Explain what happens to current when the light becomes brighter.

**Mark guidance:** I = V/R (1) = 6.0/1500 = 0.0040 A (1). LDR resistance decreases (1), so total R decreases and current increases (1).

## Anticipated misconception

A thermistor's effect here is the NTC type: warmer means less resistance.

## Assessment and next step

Collect one sentence of reasoning and one complete calculation or graph label. Start the next lesson by reteaching the most common error; students who are secure should tackle a changed numerical context.
