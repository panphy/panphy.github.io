# Lesson 2: Energy per coulomb

**Unit:** Electric Circuits · Year 10 · AQA GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy (Higher) / separate Physics
**Specification:** 6.2.1.3, 6.2.4.2
**Length:** 50 minutes

## Why this lesson is here

The same charge can give a lamp more energy when the supply p.d. is higher. Voltage describes energy per coulomb, not the speed of the charges.

## Learning objective

Explain p.d. as energy transferred per coulomb and calculate E = QV.

## Core teaching point

Potential difference is energy transferred per unit charge: 1 V = 1 J/C. A voltmeter is connected in parallel across the component.

## Equipment and preparation

Cells, two resistors or lamps, ammeter, voltmeter; PhET lab; virtual-lab worksheet pp. 7-11.

## Lesson sequence

| Minutes | Phase | Teacher move and evidence to collect |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-5 | Retrieval | Compare charge, current and energy units. |
| 5-15 | Model | Teach p.d. as J/C and correct meter placement. |
| 15-32 | Virtual Lab 2 | Measure source and component p.d.s; check the sum. |
| 32-43 | Practice | Tasks 1, 2 and 4, then explain a voltmeter reading in words. |
| 43-50 | Check | Mark four-mark question and identify unit errors. |

## Workbook task guidance

### Task 1

Draw a voltmeter across a lamp and an ammeter in series with it. Explain each position.

**Expected:** Ammeter in series, voltmeter parallel across lamp.

### Task 2

Predict how the p.d. across a single lamp changes when the battery changes from 3 V to 6 V.

**Expected:** The p.d. across the only lamp approximately doubles, provided the supply is ideal.

### Task 3

Virtual Lab 2: complete the p.d. and energy investigation on existing worksheet pp. 7-11. Record p.d. across the battery and two series components; test whether the drops add.

**Expected:** Battery p.d. approximately equals the sum of series component p.d.s; readings may differ slightly due to rounding/internal resistance.

### Task 4

Calculate energy transferred when 25 C passes through a component with 4.0 V across it.

**Expected:** E = QV = 25 x 4.0 = 100 J.

## Worked example

A lamp transfers 36 J as 6.0 C passes. V = E/Q = 36/6.0 = 6.0 V.

## AQA-style checkpoint

[4 marks] A 9.0 V battery moves 20 C of charge through a circuit. Calculate the energy transferred by the battery. Explain what a voltmeter reading of 3.0 V across one resistor means.

**Mark guidance:** E = QV (1) = 20 x 9.0 = 180 J (1). 3.0 V means 3.0 J transferred per coulomb (2).

## Anticipated misconception

Voltage is not 'used up': p.d. measures energy transfer between two points.

## Assessment and next step

Collect one sentence of reasoning and one complete calculation or graph label. Start the next lesson by reteaching the most common error; students who are secure should tackle a changed numerical context.
