# Lesson 6: Resistance in combinations

**Unit:** Electric Circuits · Year 10 · AQA GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy (Higher) / separate Physics
**Specification:** RP 15 / RP 3
**Length:** 50 minutes

## Why this lesson is here

Adding a second resistor changes the current drawn from the same supply. The direction of change depends on the wiring.

## Learning objective

Measure equivalent resistance in two arrangements and explain the results.

## Core teaching point

Measure source V and total I for one resistor, two in series and two in parallel. Rtotal = V/I each time; series is larger and parallel is smaller than one resistor.

## Equipment and preparation

Low-voltage supply, two identical resistors, switch, ammeter, voltmeter, leads; existing Resistance worksheet pp. 5-8.

## Lesson sequence

| Minutes | Phase | Teacher move and evidence to collect |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-6 | Predict | Rank one, series and parallel by current and resistance. |
| 6-13 | Set up | Model source V and total I meter positions. |
| 13-32 | Required practical B | Measure all three layouts; swap roles. |
| 32-43 | Explain | Calculate and account for the ranking. |
| 43-50 | Check | Five-mark question; correct parallel reasoning. |

## Workbook task guidance

### Task 1

Draw all three arrangements and predict the ranking of their total currents.

**Expected:** For identical resistors: series current smallest, single middle, parallel largest.

### Task 2

Build each low-voltage circuit. Measure source p.d. and total current; calculate Rtotal in the table.

**Expected:** Use measured source V and supply I; calculate V/I for each configuration. Exact values depend on the resistors.

### Task 3

Explain why the two-series value is higher and the two-parallel value is lower than a single resistor.

**Expected:** Series puts opposition on the same path; parallel provides another path, increasing total I at fixed V.

### Task 4

Evaluate: identify a source of measurement uncertainty and a change that would improve the comparison.

**Expected:** Examples: meter resolution/contact resistance; repeat readings, tight connections, same supply and temperature.

## Worked example

At 6.0 V: one 10 ohm resistor draws 0.60 A; two series draw 0.30 A, so Rtotal = 20 ohm; two parallel draw 1.20 A, so Rtotal = 5.0 ohm.

## AQA-style checkpoint

[5 marks] Two identical resistors each have resistance 12 ohm. Find their series resistance. A student measures 6.0 ohm for their parallel combination. Explain why this is plausible and state one control for a fair comparison.

**Mark guidance:** Series = 12 + 12 = 24 ohm (2). Parallel adds a path, so more total current at the same p.d. and lower total R (2); same supply p.d./same resistors/temperature (1).

## Anticipated misconception

The reciprocal formula for parallel resistance is beyond this AQA unit; understand the qualitative rule.

## Assessment and next step

Collect one sentence of reasoning and one complete calculation or graph label. Start the next lesson by reteaching the most common error; students who are secure should tackle a changed numerical context.
