# Lesson 3: Resistance and Ohm's law

**Unit:** Electric Circuits · Year 10 · AQA GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy (Higher) / separate Physics
**Specification:** 6.2.1.3-4
**Length:** 50 minutes

## Why this lesson is here

Two devices on the same supply draw different currents. Their resistances differ: R = V/I compares p.d. with current.

## Learning objective

Calculate resistance and explain why a fixed resistor can obey V = IR.

## Core teaching point

For a fixed resistor at constant temperature, current is directly proportional to p.d. Resistance is measured in ohms (ohm). Heating can change resistance.

## Equipment and preparation

PhET lab; virtual-lab worksheet pp. 12-16; low-voltage supply, fixed resistors and meters for demonstration.

## Lesson sequence

| Minutes | Phase | Teacher move and evidence to collect |
|---:|---|---|
| 0-5 | Retrieval | Convert milliampere to ampere. |

## Workbook task guidance

### Task 1

Calculate R for (a) 12 V, 0.60 A; (b) 3.0 V, 0.15 A. State the unit.

**Expected:** (a) 20 ohm; (b) 20 ohm.

### Task 2

Explain why the phrase 'a resistor obeys Ohm's law' needs the words 'at constant temperature'.

**Expected:** A fixed resistor has constant R only if its temperature is held constant.

### Task 3

Virtual Lab 3: complete the resistance investigation on existing worksheet pp. 12-16. Change one variable at a time and compare calculated R values.

**Expected:** Calculated R should remain near constant for the same fixed resistor at the same temperature; explain discrepancies with measurement and heating.

### Task 4

Sketch current (y) against p.d. (x) for a fixed resistor and state what the gradient tells you qualitatively.

**Expected:** Straight line through the origin; with I on y and V on x, steeper means smaller R.

## Worked example

A resistor has 6.0 V across it and 0.30 A through it. R = V/I = 6.0/0.30 = 20 ohm.

## AQA-style checkpoint

[4 marks] A fixed resistor has 2.0 V across it and carries 0.10 A. Calculate its resistance and predict the current at 6.0 V if its temperature stays constant.

**Mark guidance:** R = V/I (1) = 2.0/0.10 = 20 ohm (1). I = V/R (1) = 6.0/20 = 0.30 A (1).

## Anticipated misconception

A larger current does not always mean lower resistance: compare at the same p.d. and temperature.

## Assessment and next step

Collect one sentence of reasoning and one complete calculation or graph label. Start the next lesson by reteaching the most common error; students who are secure should tackle a changed numerical context.
