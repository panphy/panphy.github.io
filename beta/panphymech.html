Example experiments

    Using PanPhyMech

    Build

    • Click a part, then click the scene. Drag a part in to drop it where you want. Hold Shift to place several.
    • Drag an object to move it. Use the round handle above it to rotate and the square handle to resize.
    • Hinged and glued objects move together while you edit.
    • Right-click an object or connector, or touch and hold it, for its main settings. The small cross deletes the selection.
    • Drag empty space to pan; scroll, pinch or use + and − to zoom.

    Connect

    • Spring, rod, rope: click one end, then the other. An end on empty space is fixed to the background. Ends snap to an object’s center when you click near it.
    • Hinge, motor, glue: click where two objects overlap. On a single object, the hinge pins it to the background.
    • A spring starts at its natural length; change the length or k in the inspector.
    • Drag a spring, rod or rope by its line to move it, or select it and drag an end handle to move one end. Each end attaches to whatever is under it when you let go. Drag a hinge, motor or glue joint to a new spot the same way.

    Run and measure

    • Play (Space) runs time; Reset returns to the start. Editing after time has run makes the current state the new start.
    • While it runs, drag an object to pull it.
    • The graph shows position, velocity or energy of the chosen object, a spring, or the whole system. Hover to read values. Export as CSV or open in PanPhyPlot to fit curves.

    The physics

    • Rigid-body mechanics from Planck.js (Box2D), stepped at 240 Hz or faster for stiff springs. Springs follow Hooke’s law exactly; air resistance is \(F = kv^2\).
    • Friction between two surfaces is \(\sqrt{\mu_1 \mu_2}\): the same \(\mu\) when both match, zero if either is frictionless. Bounciness uses the larger restitution of the pair.
    • Gravitational energy is measured from y = 0 (the floor). Collisions are slightly soft, so expect small energy changes at impacts.
