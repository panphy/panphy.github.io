α · AMERICIUM-241

The nucleus throws out a helium nucleus.

An alpha particle is two protons and two neutrons: a helium nucleus. The nucleus loses 4 from its mass number and 2 from its atomic number, so it becomes a different element. Americium-241 is the source inside many smoke alarms.

Your observation mission

Press “Decay this nucleus”. Watch two protons and two neutrons group together and leave. Then read the equation: the top numbers and the bottom numbers both still add up. The new nucleus recoils a little the other way.

What does it mean?

Alpha particles are large and have a +2 charge, so they collide with many atoms and are strongly ionising. That also means they lose energy fast: a few centimetres of air or a sheet of paper stops them. Try “Test penetration”.

A model is an explanation, not a photograph.

Protons and neutrons are drawn as coloured balls packed together; real nucleons are not hard spheres, and colours and sizes are chosen to help you see the ideas. A nucleus is about 1/100 000 of an atom’s width; the electrons around the atom are not shown. Every motion is slowed down enormously: real alpha particles leave at about 5% of the speed of light, beta particles at up to 99%, and gamma rays travel at the speed of light.

“Decay this nucleus” makes one nucleus decay on demand so that you can watch it. Real decay is random: nothing can make a particular nucleus decay, or predict when it will. Americium-241 has a half-life of 432 years, carbon-14 5730 years and technetium-99m 6.0 hours. In the half-life view, one half-life always takes a few seconds on screen, whatever the isotope. Beta-minus decay also releases an antineutrino, which is beyond GCSE; it is drawn faintly. Technetium-99m emits almost only gamma rays, a reason it is chosen as a medical tracer.

Penetration ranges are typical classroom values: alpha is stopped by a sheet of paper or a few centimetres of air; beta by a few millimetres of aluminium; gamma is reduced, but never stopped completely, by several centimetres of lead. In the test, a quarter of the gamma rays get through the lead to show this; the real fraction depends on the thickness.
