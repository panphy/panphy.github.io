# PanPhyMech

A 2D mechanics sandbox in the style of Algodoo. Entry point: `sandboxes/panphymech.html`, listed under Sandboxes on the homepage. Plain HTML, CSS and classic scripts on the global `PPM` namespace; no build step. It works offline: runtime files and the manual are listed in `ASSETS_TO_CACHE` in `sw.js` and `OFFLINE_CARD_REQUIREMENTS` in `index.html`. Add new runtime files to both and bump `BUILD_ID`.

Objects (ball, box, plank, polygon, ramp, wall) and connectors (spring, rod, rope, pulley, hinge, motor, glue) go on a canvas in SI units; a spring can join another connector end to end. Play runs the physics; the graph shows position, velocity or energy of one object, a spring or the whole system, with CSV and PanPhyPlot export.

The [user manual](manual.html) has a five-step pendulum walkthrough and a reference for building, connectors, graphs, files and shortcuts. The app’s Manual control opens it in a large overlay, full-screen on small screens, with an Open in new tab option.

## Structure

| File | Role |
| --- | --- |

| `js/physics.js` | Builds a Planck world from the scene; springs, pulleys, explicit quadratic drag and implicit linear drag; readings and energies |
| `examples/` | Example experiments as ordinary `.ppmech` files; `index.json` lists them in menu order with their text (`plain` is the version without math), the object and graph group to show (`track`), and an optional view box |
| `js/svg.js` | Vector drawing context for SVG and PanPhyMD scene exports |
| `js/render.js` | Canvas drawing: grid, floor, objects, connectors, trails, vectors, handles |
| `js/editor.js` | Pointer interaction: placing, connecting, moving, rotating, resizing, moving and re-attaching connectors, the delete cross, right-click and touch-and-hold settings, grabbing, panning, zooming |
| `js/graph.js` | The sample recorder, the time graph, CSV and PanPhyPlot export |
| `js/ui.js`, `js/app.js` | Panels, inspector and quick settings; state, run loop, undo, autosave, fitting the view, files and shortcuts |

Adding an example: build it in the app, save it as a `.ppmech` file into `examples/`, add an entry to `examples/index.json`, and add the file to `ASSETS_TO_CACHE` in `/sw.js` and to this app's offline requirements in `/index.html`. `tests/examples.cjs` loads the files for the tests, so every example is also run by them.

Run the physics checks with `node --test sandboxes/panphymech/tests/*.test.cjs`. They compare free fall, projectile range, spring and pendulum periods, an elastic collision, a Newton's cradle, a block on a ramp and terminal velocity with textbook results, and run every example. `tests/pulley.test.cjs` checks the Atwood machine and cart-and-hanging-mass examples against their textbook acceleration and tension, equal masses at rest, slack and re-tightening, off-center attachment, and saving. `tests/joins.test.cjs` checks joined pairs against textbook results (series-spring period, bungee rest point, spring balance on a pulley) and their saving, deleting, copying and refitting. Export checks cover vector output and escaped labels.

Shift-drag from empty space (or a plain drag with Select several on) draws a selection box that adds the objects wholly inside it; Ctrl+A selects everything. `S.boxContents` also returns the connector ends fixed to the background and the pulley wheels inside the box; the app keeps them in `app.anchors` (cleared by any new selection), and dragging or nudging the selected objects passes them to `S.transformBodies`, so a build moves with its pivots and wheels. Dragging a connector attached to the selection (an end on a selected object or caught by the box, or a joined partner of one) moves the group too; a click without a drag selects that connector. Copy and paste selected objects or connectors with Ctrl+C / Ctrl+V (Command on Mac). Object groups retain internal connectors; connector-only copies retain their original attachments. Copies use fresh IDs and paste as one undoable edit. Mirror (M) reflects the selected object and its hinged or glued group left to right about the object’s center, flipping shapes, attachment points, starting velocities and motor directions; connectors to other objects keep their outside ends, and rods, ropes and pulleys refit their lengths. With Snap on, object placement and dragging prioritize nearby surface contact, then an object's center landing on another object's edge (`centerSnap`), over the grid, including rotated and custom shapes, and connector endpoints snap to nearby edges as well as centers. Holding Ctrl or Command pauses Snap until it is released, also mid-drag, and the Snap button shows off meanwhile.

Drag any library part directly into the scene. A completed object drop exits placement mode, including when that part was already selected, so the next click selects or moves it instead of placing a second object. Connector drags show the snapped starting point on the canvas, without a floating connector icon. Springs, rods and ropes start their first endpoint at the drop position; click the second endpoint to finish, or press Esc to cancel. A pulley's drop places its wheel; then click the rope's two ends. Hinges, motors and glue attach at the drop position. Right-click or hold an object, connector or selected connector endpoint for quick settings (500 ms for touch/pen); movement and a second finger cancel the hold.

The graph uses `/assets/graph-range.js` for mouse, touch and pen time selection. Drag across the graph to zoom into a range; CSV and PanPhyPlot exports use only that range, retaining the original sample times. **Full Graph** is a separate view-reset control before the export pills; it restores the complete recording and is disabled when the full recording is already visible. Hover over the graph to mark the nearest recorded sample and read its time and values. Keyboard +/− zoom, arrows pan, and Escape restores the full graph. A reset or new scene clears the selection.

The Terminal velocity example drops two identical 0.5 kg balls through 6 m: the coral ball overrides World drag with None, while the blue ball inherits quadratic drag tuned for a 3 m/s terminal velocity. It reaches 99% of that velocity after about 1.8 m. The example graphs the blue ball’s vertical velocity (vx, which stays 0, starts hidden); switch Graph of to compare the coral ball. Both have zero restitution to stop on landing.

## Physics model

- The scene is the setup at t = 0. Play builds a world from it; Reset rebuilds it. Editing the setup after time has run (moving, resizing, adding or deleting anything) first makes the current state the new start, with one undo step back to the old one. Parameters (mass, friction, restitution, drag, spring and motor settings, rope lengths, gravity) apply immediately without restarting.
- World **Accuracy** (`world.accuracy`: `standard`, `fine` or `veryFine`, saved with the scene) sets the longest step: 1/240, 1/480 or 1/1000 s. Solid rods and ropes halve it, and it is shortened so the stiffest spring has ω·h ≤ 0.05 (not below 1/5000 s). One step serves the whole scene and is recomputed whenever parameters sync. A non-Standard accuracy is listed in the notes pill.
- The reset icon in the World card restores all world settings to the new-scene defaults in one undoable edit. Parameters apply live; restoring a disabled floor follows the usual floor edit behavior and makes the current state the new start.
- Springs follow Hooke's law with optional linear damping; air resistance is F = kv². Both are applied as forces before each step, so their energy bookkeeping is exact up to the integrator.
- Box2D keeps a 1 cm skin round polygons. `physics.js` insets each object's outline (before splitting merged or cut shapes into convex pieces, so seams stay smooth) and the floor by that skin less a quarter of `linearSlop` (and sets mass, center and inertia from the drawn shape), so objects rest flush on their drawn outlines, within about 1 mm, and an object snapped into contact stays put when time starts. Balls are not inset, so their rolling radius stays exact.
- Planck mixes friction as √(μ₁μ₂) and restitution as the larger of the pair. Box2D adds a step's gravity before applying restitution, so every bounce would gain about g·h of speed; a `pre-solve` handler scales each contact's restitution to reflect the approach speed without it. That also gives resting contacts no bounce, so `velocityThreshold` (contacts closing slower than this are inelastic) is only 0.01 m/s. Sleeping is off, so slow motion is never frozen.
- Ideal rods and ropes lose energy to the integrator: each step moves their ends in straight lines, and the joint then deletes the outward part of the velocity. After each step `keepLineSpeeds` turns that part back into speed along the swing, so a pendulum keeps its energy to about 1% at any accuracy. Hinges and solid connectors do not get this correction.
- `sim.forcesOn(id)` lists the forces on a moving object in the latest step, each with its point of action: weight at the center of mass, normal reaction and friction from `post-solve` contact impulses, spring force and tension where the connector meets the object's outline (a ray cast in from its other end, or from a solid connector's first joint outside the object), joint reactions at the joint, and drag. Before time starts it reads them from a probe copy of the world advanced one step (dropped when a live parameter changes). `renderer.forceArrows(scale)` turns them into world-space arrows for the selected objects, or every moving object when none is selected, at one scale (the heaviest shown object's weight is 1.2 times its on-screen size, 60–160 px); the canvas draws them in one color per force (`--force-*` tokens), and Fit widens its box to hold them. Two wheels driven at one speed through a rigid chassis leave the split of friction between them undetermined, so the Motor car example drives only its back wheel.
- Pulley (`kind: "pulley"`): a fixed, frictionless, massless wheel (center `px`, `py`, radius `r`) and one massless, inextensible rope of total length `length` from end `a` over the wheel to end `b`. `S.pulleyGeometry` is the one geometry for physics, drawing and editing: each straight part runs from its end to a tangent point on the wheel, and the length is both straight parts plus the wrap, `r` × angle. The rope wraps round the far side of the wheel from the narrower gap between the ends (`S.pulleyWinding`, over the top for two hanging ends); the simulation keeps the side it starts with and follows the wrap angle continuously from step to step, so an end swung over the top takes the rope off the wheel (straight from end to end, `offWheel`) instead of making its length jump by a whole turn; it does not re-wrap onto the other side. `physics.js` builds a Planck `PulleyJoint` and replaces its three solver steps with a rope's: the tension impulse is clamped to pull only, a slack rope gets Box2D's rope-joint speculative term (so it is caught in the step it would overshoot, without changing velocities by hand), and position correction acts only on an overlong rope. The length's rate of change is `ua·va + ub·vb` (each end's velocity along its straight part), so off-center ends also feel a torque. The wheel is a static circle that objects collide with; the rope collides with nothing. Rope length applies live (`updateLink`); wheel position and radius are setup. Editing keeps the rope taut, as for ropes, and refuses an end inside the wheel or a wheel over a movable object (`S.pulleyProblem`). Limits: one fixed wheel and one rope; no movable or compound pulleys, wheel inertia or friction, or wrapping round other objects; swinging sides do not get the `keepLineSpeeds` energy correction.
- Joined connectors: a spring end may join one background end of another spring, ideal rope or rod, or a pulley's rope. The two ends hold each other's id instead of a body id, and the join is a massless knot, so each pair is solved as one force element between its outer ends (`S.pairGeometry`): springs in series give k₁k₂/(k₁ + k₂); spring + rope pulls only when taut; spring + rod pushes too; spring + pulley makes the pulley rope elastic (no `PulleyJoint`; the sim keeps the winding and turn per pair). The knot's position is always derived, never simulated, so springs joined to springs have no damping (it would need a state of its own). `S.endPoint` resolves a joined end to the knot for drawing, editing and readings; a joined end's `la`/`lb` keeps its last setup position as a fallback. `S.tidyLinks` (after every edit and on load) turns a join whose partner is gone into a background end, and removes connectors left with no end on an object. Only one join per connector and no chains or knots of three; joined rods and ropes cannot be solid. Files are format version 2; older copies of the app skip joined pairs.
- Joints: rod = rigid distance joint, rope = rope joint (maximum length), hinge and motor = revolute joint (link end `a` turns relative to `b`; positive speed is counterclockwise; the inspector sets a direction, stored in `dir`, and a speed size, and `speed` keeps the signed value), glue = weld joint. A connector end with no object is fixed to the background at a world point.
- Rods and ropes are ideal constraints unless `solid` is set. Objects they join still collide with each other (`collideConnected`); only hinges and glue let joined parts overlap. A solid rod is one thin box (6 cm) hinged at both ends; a solid rope is a chain of 3 cm boxes about 12 cm long, hinged end to end, in one collision group so its links do not collide with each other. Rope joints from each link to both ends cap every point's distance at its length along the rope, which stops the chain stretching under load. Scenes with solid connectors step at 1/480 s with more solver passes. Their mass (`mass`) counts in the system energy and in the tension reading, which sums every joint at end `a`.
- World `dragModel` chooses `none` (default), `linear` or `quadratic`. New objects use `dragModel: "world"`; an object's explicit choice replaces World drag for that object only. Only one drag law acts on an object. Solid rods and ropes inherit World drag, sharing the total coefficient across their segments.
- Linear drag uses `linearDrag` (b, 0–1000 kg/s) or `linearDragSource: "stokes"` with `viscosity` (η, 0–1 Pa·s). Stokes calculates b = 6πrη using the ball radius or an equal-area radius for other shapes. Planck integrates linear damping implicitly as b/m. Quadratic drag uses `drag` (k, 0–50 kg/m), with its existing step limit. None of these models infer streamlining or add buoyancy or rotational drag. Model and coefficient edits apply live and undo without resetting time.
- Movable objects with explicit drag overrides have a steady amber halo, an Inspector note and an entry in the notes pill at the top right of the scene. Clicking a note selects the object and focuses its drag control; choosing World removes the override cues. Fixed objects have no active drag or halo. PNG/SVG/PanPhyMD exports omit the halo as an editing cue.
- Old files with viscosity migrate to World Linear/Stokes; old per-object quadratic coefficients migrate to explicit Quadratic overrides. If both were active, the object override now replaces World drag, matching the new model, and is identified by a note. Other old objects inherit World. New files retain each model and its inactive parameter values.
- Gravitational energy is m g (y − y₀), with y₀ set in the Gravity section of the World card (0 by default). Energies cover moving objects only.
- The recorder samples every moving object and spring each 0.01 s of simulated time, halving its rate after 24 000 samples, so the graph can switch objects after a run.

## Merged and cut shapes

Merge and Subtract turn selected objects into a `custom` object whose `paths` hold its outline: polygons of `[x, y]` rings (outer ring first, then holes), in local coordinates about the centroid. Circles become 24–64-sided polygons. For the engine, each outline is triangulated with earcut and neighbouring triangles are merged back into convex pieces of at most 8 corners (Hertel–Mehlhorn); every piece is a fixture of one rigid body, with density = mass / area. Slivers Planck cannot build (corners closer than half its `linearSlop`) are left out rather than replaced by its default 2 m box. A merged shape takes the total mass and the first object's settings, and is fixed if any part was. Subtract cuts the object picked last out of each of the others and removes it; a part that splits apart becomes several objects sharing its mass by area. Connector ends keep their world positions on the new shapes. Objects can catch slightly on the seams between pieces, and very thin walls can let fast objects through.

## Storage

The scene autosaves to `panphy-mech-scene-v1` and display choices to `panphy-mech-prefs-v1` in browser storage. Files are `.ppmech` JSON with `format: "panphy-mech"`. Save opens the browser’s filename/location picker where available and asks for a filename before downloading elsewhere. The File menu exports the current view as PNG or vector SVG, or sends an embedded SVG image to PanPhyMD; SVG exports omit editing controls and keep visible trails, labels and vectors.

Fit frames the current object positions and connector anchors with the original margins and floor placement. Objects far from the rest are left out and named so a runaway object cannot shrink the scene to dots.


The manual overlay uses `/assets/manual-overlay.js` and `/assets/manual-overlay.css`. It loads the standalone guide on first use and keeps it mounted so reading position and expanded topics survive closing and reopening. Escape closes the guide after any open Contents or screenshot dialog; closing restores focus to Manual. Same-tab links back to the app close the guide; explicit new-tab links and links to other pages open separately to preserve the workspace.
